# Contractor Terms: Balance, Breach, Third-Party Tools, and Exit

This document states the agreed terms for balanced obligations, breach
notification, third-party dependencies, and data portability. It complements the
statement of work; if they conflict, the statement of work wins for scope and
this document wins for process.

## 1. Balanced Obligations

- Neither party is required to accept unlimited liability. Each party is
  responsible for its own acts and omissions.
- Stardial provides access, feedback, and decisions within the timeframes in the
  statement of work; the contractor provides the agreed deliverables.
- Delay caused by one party extends the other party's deadlines by the same
  amount of time.
- The contractor's total liability is capped at the fees paid under the
  engagement, except for fraud, wilful misconduct, or breach of confidentiality.
- Neither party is liable for indirect or consequential losses.

## 2. Breach Notification and Cure

1. A party that becomes aware of a material breach must notify the other in
   writing (email to the contact named in the statement of work, or a GitHub
   issue) within **5 business days**.
2. The notice must say what the breach is, when it started, and what is being
   done about it.
3. The breaching party has **10 business days** to cure the breach (the "cure
   period"). Deadlines affected by the breach pause during the cure period.
4. If the breach is not cured in time, the other party may terminate the
   engagement for cause with written notice, and pay only for work accepted so
   far.
5. Security incidents follow the shorter **24-hour** reporting window in the
   security terms.

## 3. Third-Party Tools and Licenses

- Stardial provides and pays for accounts and licenses it requires the
  contractor to use (repository access, hosting, paid APIs).
- The contractor provides and pays for their own general tooling (editor, local
  machine, general-purpose software).
- Open-source dependencies must use licenses compatible with the project's
  license. The contractor must flag any new dependency that is not.
- Third-party vendors retain ownership of their tools. Ownership of integration
  work created specifically as an engagement deliverable follows section 4;
  approved pre-existing materials and third-party components remain subject to
  the ownership and licence terms in
  [Contractor Terms: Scope, Compliance, Disputes, and Exit](./contractor-terms-commercial-and-legal.md#16-pre-existing-materials-and-intellectual-property).
  The vendor is responsible for support of its own tool.
- If a third-party tool fails or changes terms, the contractor must propose a
  replacement or workaround within **5 business days** at no extra cost.

## 4. Data and Access Portability

- Work product created specifically for the engagement (including code, designs,
  documents, and credentials created for it) belongs to Stardial, subject to the
  retained ownership and licences for approved Background Materials and
  third-party components in
  [Contractor Terms: Scope, Compliance, Disputes, and Exit](./contractor-terms-commercial-and-legal.md#16-pre-existing-materials-and-intellectual-property).
  The contractor assigns to Stardial all rights it holds in that work product
  and must obtain equivalent assignments from its personnel and subcontractors.
- Stardial keeps administrator access to every system used for the engagement
  and can export its data at any time, including during the engagement.
- On request or within **10 business days** of the engagement ending, the
  contractor must hand over all work product, documentation needed to run it,
  and any credentials, then delete their copies.
- The contractor provides reasonable transition assistance (up to **5 business
  days**) to hand over to a replacement, at the agreed rate.
- No vendor lock-in: deliverables must not depend on a tool the contractor
  controls exclusively.
